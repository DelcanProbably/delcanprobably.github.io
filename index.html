quantum loop

Quantum Loop was my first commercially-released game, published on Steam in November 2025, and it's one of my proudest achievements. Leading up to release I was given the opportunity to showcase at PAX Aus, SXSW Sydney, and Steam Next Fest! I made Quantum Loop almost entirely solo, using a custom engine based on the PyGame framework for Python.

It's a non-linear adventure game in a time loop, all about problem-solving by exploiting your knowledge of the world. With no quest log or specific directions, there's a big emphasis on player choice and freedom. Objectives can be completed in a multitude of ways that aren't explicitly stated.

I started Quantum Loop in January 2024 as a passion project, and after over a year and a half of development shipped to Steam on 26 November 2025. I made almost everything; a couple of my talented friends helped with the promotional art and about half of the soundtrack. I ran regular playtests across development, and put to use everything I've learnt about iterative design as the game concept gradually morphed from a simple space-sim into the unique time loop gameplay it has today.

Releasing Quantum Loop was one of the most incredible experiences I've ever had. I learnt so much about managing large-scale game projects and getting a game ready to ship, and I'm immensely proud of how it turned out.

Remote Object System

A system I designed for Unity projects to communicate with Raspberry Pi devices over a local network. I created a simple protocol to network a host device to many child devices. I primarily experimented with audio, making external physical devices play sounds remotely to reflect gameplay. I also implemented interfacing with GPIO, allowing you to control LEDs and other basic electronics. The goal was to allow developers to cheaply and easily use off-the-shelf electronics to create a novel physical representation of their games for presentations and showcases.

The documentation is pretty rock-solid and I break down all the systems at play pretty thoroughly. From completing this project, I learnt a lot about networking and problem-solving through iterative development. There's a a rough demo video available here.

My research

For my master's research, I looked into practical accessibility considerations included in mainstream gaming. I performed an analysis of games currently on the market to determine which features are commonly included, and where there are gaps. I also investigated the technical back-end implementation of accessibility configuration features.

After recently getting my first paper acceptance, I will soon have an academic publication in the game accessibility field!

My thesis has been submitted and should be publicly available soon!

Game accessibility advice

I'm open for freelance work giving accessible game design advice and consulting. From examining a demo of your game, I can give practical advice on various categories, including subtitles, controls, colour blindness considerations, visual clarity, and more. My research in the field has given me a deep understanding of the basic needs of accessibility and surrounding guidelines, and I can quickly identify standard features that are missing and areas of your game requiring adjustment.

My experience as an independent developer gives me unique first-hand insights into the practical difficulties and scope creep that can be involved with including effective accessibility. I can take into account your project's state and suggest the best steps your team can take to make your game playable by as many people as possible.

My number one suggestion is always to get people involved in your game who have the needs you want to ensure you cater for, either through playtesting or directly in the design process. This is a reliable method to improve your accessibility and is recommended by academic sources. I can find basic issues and give your team direction, but in most cases cannot give a personal perspective, and will therefore always recommend playtesting to solve these issues more deeply.

I'll be posting more about my learnings from accessibility work on previous projects soon, along with findings from my research!

If you're interested in getting me involved with your project, shoot me an email!

Game audio

My audio background often aligns with my game development projects! Quantum Loop uses a custom game engine, and required me to write my own systems for juggling audio channels to get a clean, crackle-free output without sounds muting each other, and in a way that was elegant to develop with. Gambit's Unity audio mixing system was made by me, and taught me about how to effectively use ducking and compression on a game with many audio sources. Through Remote Audio System, I learnt how to create low-latency audio when sending messages over a local network, and explored the usage of audio in immersive experiences.

I have ample experience in sound selection and sample manipulation; for my own games I often hunt down sounds with appropriate usage rights online, and then use my audio production skills to layer and process them into something that fits just right. In Quantum Loop and many smaller games, I designed my own electronic sounds using both software and hardware synthesisers and processing techniques. Although I don't have as much experience, I'm very interested in audio recording and microphone techniques, and hope to explore this area more in future.
